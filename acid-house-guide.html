303BOX / LEARN BY DOING

Turn one loop into a sixteen-bar performance.

Change entrance timing and layers instead of adding more bass notes. You will leave with a drum map and a four-part performance plan you can rehearse by hand.

1. Separate beats from steps

In this 4/4 exercise, one bar contains sixteen equal steps. The main beats land on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13. At 120 BPM each step is 125 ms and each bar is two seconds. 303box repeats the same sixteen steps; it does not automatically chain the sixteen-bar plan below.

The D minor example starts on step 2, one sixteenth after the first kick. For the comparison, move only that D to step 1. Leave all later notes, accents and slides in place.

A separates the kick and bass entrance; B puts their first attacks together. Try the space in A with a dense kick, or B for a more unified entrance. These are arrangement choices, not a universal ranking. The recordings above contain bass only, without drums.

3. Enter a three-voice drum map

Save an important rhythm with DOWNLOAD first. Clear the rhythm section and activate only the steps below. Leave all other steps and drum voices off. Loading a bass example preserves the rhythm grid, so enter this part manually.

Drum placement
VoiceActive stepsRole in this exercise
BD / Kick1, 5, 9, 13A reference on every main beat
CP / Clap5, 13Emphasis on beats two and four
CH / Closed hi-hat3, 7, 11, 15An answer between the main beats

Listen to the rhythm alone before adding bass. Keep SD, TM and OH off initially so extra hits do not obscure the kick/bass relationship. Balance levels by ear; turn down the dominant part rather than pushing every part louder.

4. Rehearse four sections

Sixteen bars: about 32 seconds at 120 BPM
BarsAudible layersAction
1–4Kick + clapLeave bass stopped and CH steps off.
5–8Kick + clap + bassUse 303 PLAY at the start of bar 5 to bring in your chosen bass.
9–12Kick + clap + bass + CHEnable the prepared CH hits; keep bass notes fixed.
13–16Kick + clap + CHUse 303 STOP at bar 13 to remove bass while rhythm continues.

Rehearse each section separately with playback stopped first. Enabling multiple CH steps by hand may span a bar; 303box has no automatic scene transitions for this plan. For precisely recorded transitions, record separate sections or arrange them in a DAW.

5. Evaluate the arrangement with three questions

  1. Does the bass entrance at bar 5 establish a new section? If not, compare kick and bass levels before adding more notes.
  2. Does adding hi-hat at bar 9 increase motion? If it feels crowded, try steps 7 and 15 instead of all four hits.
  3. Is the bass removal at bar 13 noticeable? If little changes, the bass may already have been too quiet in the previous section.

Write down bass choice A or B, the drum steps and the transition bars. The result is a repeatable performance sketch, not a finished song. For note entry, use the pattern workshop. To play an external device, follow the live MIDI connection guide.
